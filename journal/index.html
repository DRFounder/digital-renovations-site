Digital Renovations Journal

Progress, decisions, and lessons from the mission.

A public record of meaningful milestones shaping Digital Renovations and the Veteran’s Companion—shared deliberately, without exposing private, proprietary, or security-sensitive work.

Selected public record

Development milestones

This public journal is curated from Digital Renovations’ internal master record. Private, proprietary, security-sensitive, and unconfirmed material is not published here.

March 9, 2025 Veteran’s Companion

Veteran’s Companion work formally begins

Formal work began on the concept that would become the Veteran’s Companion. The project developed around helping veterans organize their thoughts during periods of strain while preserving dignity, autonomy, and connection to real human support.

This establishes the beginning of the project’s documented development record.

December 4, 2025 Company

Digital Renovations LLC formed

Digital Renovations LLC was established as a South Dakota domestic, member-managed limited liability company, providing an organizational home for development of the Veteran’s Companion.

The project moved from an individual concept into a formal business structure.

May 31, 2026 Project doctrine

First major project canon consolidated

The first major Veteran’s Companion canon brought the project’s mission, interaction doctrine, safety principles, privacy concepts, and development direction into a coherent controlled record.

Accumulated project thinking became an organized body of knowledge rather than a collection of disconnected discussions.

June 15–21, 2026 Federal readiness

Federal-applicant foundation established

Digital Renovations completed foundational federal-registration work, including an active SAM.gov registration, confirmed Unique Entity Identifier, and successful tax identification matching. Remaining dependencies continued to be tracked explicitly.

The company became a prospective federal small-business applicant with essential administrative infrastructure in place.

June 17, 2026 Research environment

Controlled behavioral sandbox documented

A hosted beta configuration was documented for exploring interaction behavior, distress levels, crisis boundaries, and privacy rules. It was explicitly treated as an experimental behavioral sandbox—not as the finished Veteran’s Companion product.

Safety and interaction concepts gained a practical testing environment without overstating the maturity of the intended software architecture.

June 17–21, 2026 Funding readiness

Structured funding-readiness system created

A structured readiness package was developed covering organizational preparation, federal registrations, concept materials, team needs, safety doctrine, risk management, evaluation planning, milestones, budgeting, and external support.

Grant preparation became a managed development process rather than an ad hoc writing effort.

July 2, 2026 Safety doctrine

Human Bridge Doctrine formally captured

The Veteran’s Companion Concept Capture Report documented a central principle: technology should help bridge veterans back to competent human support. Human connection—not prolonged engagement with AI—is the destination.

The doctrine established a clear philosophical and safety foundation for the project.

September 4, 2026 Public identity

Digital Renovations domain secured

Digital Renovations secured its web identity and began establishing an independent public presence. The production domain became digitalrenovations.net.

The company moved from internal infrastructure toward a durable, discoverable identity.

September 6–7, 2026 Infrastructure

Version-controlled website infrastructure established

The Digital Renovations website repository was created and configured for GitHub Pages with the custom digitalrenovations.net domain and working DNS.

The company gained an independently owned, reviewable, and version-controlled web presence.

September 2026 Brand identity

Digital Renovations visual identity refined

The DR monogram became the company’s primary mark. Earlier rustic treatments were retired from the website while the underlying identity was retained and refined.

Digital Renovations began presenting a coherent public identity rather than a temporary placeholder.

September 2026 Veteran’s Companion

Veteran’s Companion public identity developed

The Veteran’s Companion became clearly presented as Digital Renovations’ flagship initiative, with its veteran provenance expressed through the statement, “Made by a Veteran, for Veterans.”

The company and project gained a unified public philosophy centered on technology as a bridge.

September 13–15, 2026 Website

Homepage developed beyond its placeholder

The original landing page was developed into a substantive homepage presenting Digital Renovations, the Veteran’s Companion, the company mission, and its bridge-centered design language.

Review branches and pull requests were used to keep public changes controlled and inspectable.

September 16, 2026 Organization

Organizational structure established

Digital Renovations established a future-facing organizational structure covering executive leadership, technology and engineering, research and clinical participation, security and infrastructure, and business administration.

The company documented how future leadership and specialist roles can support the mission.

September 16, 2026 Governance

Accomplishment and development journal established

Digital Renovations established an internal master journal to preserve a contemporaneous record of company and Veteran’s Companion development. This public edition presents selected entries appropriate for external readers.

Future collaborators and reviewers can trace not only what the project became, but how it developed.

September 18, 2026 Organizational development

From planning to organized development

Digital Renovations reached several significant milestones this week as we continue building the organizational foundation required to develop the Veteran’s Companion responsibly.

Opening the AI/ML technical leadership position

We formally posted an opening for a Fractional Senior AI/ML Technical Lead.

This marks an important transition for the Veteran’s Companion. We have moved beyond merely identifying the need for specialized technical expertise and have begun actively building the team that will help turn the project’s principles and architecture into a technically credible development program.

The person selected for this role will help evaluate and strengthen the Veteran’s Companion’s language-model architecture, prototype strategy, safety considerations, reproducibility, evaluation methods, and overall technical feasibility. The position is intentionally fractional at this stage, allowing Digital Renovations to bring senior expertise into the project while maintaining a responsible staffing structure appropriate to its current phase.

The role is advisory and collaborative. Project mission, organizational authority, and final decisions remain with the founder, while the technical lead will be expected to examine assumptions, identify weaknesses, and contribute evidence-based recommendations. Respectful disagreement is welcome; alignment around the mission and the veteran remains the standard.

Establishing formal founder timekeeping

Digital Renovations has also established a formal system for recording the founder’s working time.

A substantial amount of research, planning, writing, architecture development, organizational work, and funding preparation has already gone into the Veteran’s Companion. Consistent timekeeping creates a more accurate record of that effort and provides a stronger foundation for project management, cost estimation, staffing decisions, and future grant administration.

The system is deliberately practical. Its purpose is to create a credible operational record without allowing administrative overhead to interfere with the work itself.

This milestone may be less visible than a new design or technical feature, but it is equally important. Responsible development requires more than a compelling idea; it requires documented effort, disciplined operations, and the ability to account for how resources are used.

Strengthening the Veteran Advisory Round Table

We also refined the staffing structure for the Veteran Companion Advisory Round Table.

The founding four veteran advisors will remain together to preserve continuity as the project develops. Their shared history with the project will allow them to evaluate new ideas against earlier discussions rather than approaching each stage without context.

Before project funding, participation will remain voluntary, with reasonable meals and related participation expenses covered by Digital Renovations. If funding is secured, compensation will be offered in recognition of the time, experience, and perspective these veterans contribute. Any advisor will be publicly identified as a contributing member only with that veteran’s consent.

Longer-term planning provides for two separate four-member Veteran Round Tables. Each group would meet independently, allowing the project to hear distinct discussions rather than blending every perspective into a single room. Together, the two groups are intended to represent the five traditional military service branches while also including three additional veteran perspectives relevant to the Veteran’s Companion’s mission.

The Round Tables will serve as advisory groups—not company employees, clinical authorities, or substitutes for formal research. Their purpose is to help ensure that veteran experience remains present when the project considers language, trust, dignity, usability, safety, and the practical realities of seeking support.

Building the structure around the mission

Taken together, these milestones represent meaningful organizational progress.

Digital Renovations is beginning formal technical recruitment, documenting the founder effort behind the project, and building continuity into veteran participation. Each action strengthens the structure surrounding the Veteran’s Companion while preserving its central principle:

Technology should serve as a bridge to human connection—not become the destination.
